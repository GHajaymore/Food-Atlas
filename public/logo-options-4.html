WikiFoodia — round four

Eleven marks so far have all been the same thing in different shapes: thin gold line-art, one weight, one colour, geometric. If none has landed, the register is the problem rather than the metaphor. These four are deliberately in four different registers — a wordmark, a knockout, a hand-drawn mark and a monogram.

M · Wordmark — the name does the work

No symbol at all. "WikiFoodia" is already a distinctive, unusual word; a mark competing with it is redundant. The only drawn detail is the double-o set as two plates — free, because your name already contains two circles. Strategic advantage: it gets stronger every time the name is written, and it cannot be copied without copying the name.

N · Knockout — solid, not drawn

A filled disc with the spoon cut out of it rather than drawn on top. Every previous mark was thin lines on a dark ground, which is why they all felt alike and all got weaker as they shrank. Solid shapes do the opposite: this is the only option that gets stronger at 16px, because a disc is still a disc when it is sixteen pixels across.

O · The W of bowls

A "W" drawn as three bowls side by side. Monogram and food in one shape, with no cleverness to explain: the letter is the vessels. Three of them rather than two because three is what the app requires before a record is authenticated — and because two bowls read as a "U".

P · Drawn by a hand, not a compass

The same bowl, drawn with an uneven line and an open seam — a thing somebody made rather than a shape a program generated. Every other mark here is geometrically perfect, and geometric perfection is what makes an atlas of home cooking feel like a database. The one register none of the eleven tried.
