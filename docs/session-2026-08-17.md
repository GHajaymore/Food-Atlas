## The architecture change that matters

**The catalogue is fetched, not bundled.** `src/data/catalogue.ts` used to `import`
four JSON files; Metro inlines a static import, so 16,000 records of prose became
24 MB of JavaScript and 20–30 seconds of parsing before first paint.

- `src/data/build.ts` — the transformation. All the rules. This is what the tests exercise.
- `src/data/catalogue.ts` — live bindings plus `loadCatalogue()`.
- `public/data/*.json` — what is served. Regenerate with `node scripts/compact-data.mjs`
  then copy `src/data/*.min.json` over `public/data/*.json`.
- `app/_layout.tsx` gates every route on the load, so the seven screens still read
  `catalogue` as a plain array.

**Native is not finished.** A device has no neighbouring `public/` folder.
`EXPO_PUBLIC_DATA_URL` supplies the origin there; web needs nothing.

## The two habits that found almost every bug

**1. Dry-run a rule before applying it.** Every deletion rule was run with `--dry`
first and the output read. This caught: `brand` prefix-matching "(Brandade de
Morue)" and "(brandy)"; "Culture of…" nearly deleting the UNESCO borscht record; an
"X of Y" rule about to remove 282 real provinces (Province of Chieti, Autonomous
Republic of Crimea).

**2. Read the app, not the data.** Three faults this session were invisible in JSON
where every field was individually true:
- The Italian cookbook told Italy its own cookbook was foreign to it.
- The place picker listed China and People's Republic of China as two countries.
- A register record printed "Nothing documents how this is made" above 899
  characters of its own production method.

## The recurring bug class — now covered by a test

Five times a script wrote something correct and the app ignored it. `sourceLanguage`
hard-coded to `'en'` in two mappings; ingredients read only from `evidence`;
`atRiskEvidence` re-derived from the recipe instead of the history; `originClaims`
written as strings into a field typed for sourced claims.

`__tests__/plumbing.test.ts` traces each field from the source row to the record
built from it. **Its first version could not fail** — it asked whether *any* record
showed a field, so deleting the at-risk plumbing left it green. It traces per record
now; reintroducing either bug turns it red, which was verified by doing exactly that.

## Sources, and what each was worth

| source | result |
|---|---|
| Wikipedia article lead images | 4,019 identified, 83% hit rate — matched by article identity, no guard needed |
| Other-language Wikipedia | 2,253 accounts in the language of the place |
| Wikipedia langlinks | 3,849 records readable elsewhere; drives the 42 offered languages |

| Wikidata P31 / P495 | 594 non-food removed, 199 origins corrected |
| Multilingual Wikibooks cookbooks | **3,537 recipes with ordered steps** (fr 1,127 · es 836 · de 682 · it 642 · pt 250) |

| Italian PAT register (open data) | 95 records with a method and 51 with equipment; CC BY 4.0 / CC0 |

**Dead ends, recorded so nobody repeats them:**
- **EU DOP/IGP register** — I said it was "a single download". It is not. eAmbrosia
  and GIview are HTML applications with no JSON; the only machine-readable EU data is
  map geometry of production areas.
- **Sicily's PAT file** — server refuses connections outright, not a timeout.
- **Social media photographs** — no free tier, and a credit line is not a licence.
  The lawful free route is the contributor publishing to Wikimedia Commons, which is
  what the contribute flow now asks for.
- **Matching a recipe to a Wikipedia article of the same name.** Measured first: 18%
  of photoless recipes have an article of exactly that title carrying a lead image.
  Then checked what it returned — 3 of the first 6 were wrong. "Arisa" (Malaysian
  blended chicken) matched an Italian singer photographed at Sanremo; "Arambasici"
  (Croatian) matched a Turkish dish; "Arjoli" (Maltese) followed a redirect to French
  aioli. An exact title match is not evidence of the same subject, and a redirect
  actively destroys it — MediaWiki says two names are one subject, not that two
  dishes are one dish. Abandoned; the six photographs were reverted and the script
  deleted rather than left as a trap.

  The mistake was in the probe, not the code: it measured *availability* and not
  *accuracy*. It answered "is there a picture" when the question was "is it the right
  picture". Any future source should be sampled for correctness before it is built.

- **~4,100 Italian PAT products** have no article in any language. One in eight has
  one anywhere. No amount of encyclopaedia scraping reaches them.

## Wikimedia, practically

One rate limit across every Wikimedia API, per IP. **Read `Retry-After`** — it says
four seconds. Guessing an escalating backoff made a four-second pause look like a
permanent outage and cost an hour of misdiagnosis plus six lost batches. Run one pass
at a time.

Two encoding traps, both real: Wikimedia appends UTF-8 tracking parameters to image
URLs (`Name.jpg?utm_source=…`), and MediaWiki returns titles with spaces where URLs
use underscores. Either one silently loses most of a run.

## What is left

**Needs a decision, not code:** translation is inert. `EXPO_PUBLIC_TRANSLATION_ENDPOINT`
is unset, so 5,108 non-English accounts can be identified but not read by someone who
does not speak the language. It needs a backend route holding an API key and a monthly
cap — the only part of this project that costs money.

**Free and available:**
- 3,657 recipes still have no photograph.
- `enrich-infobox` has rows it has not walked.
- Slow Food's Ark of Taste (~6,000 endangered foods) remains the best at-risk source
  and needs a partnership, not a scraper.

# 2026-08-19 — the name, the ask, and the money

## WikiFoodia

Renamed from *Global Taste*. The name now says what the project is — open,
collective, encyclopaedic — so the tagline stopped repeating it and says the thing
the name does not: **"How the world actually cooks."** Most food writing gives you a
convenient version; this one tries to give you what people really do, and "actually"
is the word carrying that.

Two taglines were rejected for a reason worth keeping. *"Written down by the people
who cook it"* describes a community model with no contributions in it yet. *"Before
it's lost"* leads with preservation, which is 31 records of 16,550. A tagline should
not promise what the atlas has not got.

The rename reaches `app.json` (name, slug, scheme, bundle ids) and `src/brand.ts`.
The **folder is still `global-taste`** and so is the git remote — renaming either
buys nothing and breaks paths. One live trap: the preview launch config lives in the
*Golf* project's `.claude/launch.json`, not this repo's, because the tool reads the
primary working directory; its entries are now `wikifoodia-web` / `wikifoodia-dist`.
The Expo dev server reads `app.json` **once at startup**, so a server left running
across the rename serves the old `<title>` and looks like a bug that is not there.

## Donations, and why Open Collective

A donation page is where a free project usually starts lying. "Support our servers"
is the standard line and it is false here — this runs inside a free tier and spends
nothing. An app that deletes fabricated view counts cannot then invent a budget to
ask against.

So `app/support.tsx` names three things and one of the numbers is zero: translation
(tens of dollars a month, and genuinely blocked on it), hosting (nothing so far),
the sources (nothing, and it will stay nothing). Then it says what money does *not*
buy — no badge, no ranking, no advertising — because the product's whole claim is
that classifications come from evidence, and a reader who has just been asked for
money is entitled to hear it.

**Open Collective** was chosen for the same reason the page exists: it publishes the
ledger, itemised and public, so that promise is kept by the platform rather than by
us remembering to update a paragraph. The screen links straight to `/transactions`.

Two decisions in the code that are the point of it:

- The destination is built from a **slug**, not a pasted URL, so there is one place
  it can be wrong and it is obvious when it is.
- With no slug, **there is no button** — `canAcceptDonations()` is false and the card
  says so. A donate control pointing nowhere spends a reader's goodwill on a dead
  link, which is worse than not asking.

It is inert until Ajay creates the collective and sets `EXPO_PUBLIC_OPENCOLLECTIVE`.
I do not create accounts on his behalf.

Currency is **USD**, named once in `CURRENCY` and printed in the lead rather than
written into each sentence — a page about being straight with money should not end up
quoting two currencies and meaning neither.

## Still open

The request *"use public assistant to get the authenticity of the food"* is
unresolved. A free model **judging** authenticity contradicts the rule the whole
catalogue rests on: `assess()` never infers technique or community validation,
because a model guessing "this looks authentic" produces a confident number with
nothing behind it. A model **finding and reading open sources** is a different thing
and is fine. Ajay has not said which he meant.

# 2026-08-20 — find the sources, don't let it judge

Ajay settled the open question from yesterday: an assistant may **find and read real
sources**; it may not **judge** whether a food is authentic. That line is now enforced
in code rather than remembered.

## `src/domain/sourceFinding.ts`

The rules a candidate source must pass, and — more importantly — the ceiling on what
one is allowed to contribute.

**Corroborate, not merely match.** A record already knows a country, sometimes a
region, a cuisine, some ingredients. A real source mentions at least one of them
independently. A text that names the dish and nothing else is not evidence it is the
same dish; it is evidence that two things share a word. This project has paid for that
lesson twice, most expensively when searching Commons by name illustrated a Malaysian
chicken dish with an Italian singer at Sanremo.

**An allow-list of what a finder may write**: a citation, `hasAccount`, ingredients as
text. Never a heritage designation, never the technique or community dimensions —
`assess()` keeps both at zero because nobody in the chain watched anyone cook.

That containment is what makes the thing safe to run at scale, and it is asserted by a
test rather than promised in a comment: the strongest a found source can be is
`hasAccount`, and a record with an account and nothing else is **Unverified with no
score**. A false positive can add a wrong citation. It cannot make the atlas call a
food authentic.

Scripts import this module directly — Node 24 strips the types — so the finder and the
app apply the same test to the byte. That closes, at the source, the class of bug that
bit five times: a script and an app each holding their own copy of a rule.

## The measurement, which is the useful part

English Wikisource, 77 records searched: **one** candidate survived every rule, and it
was Presidential Proclamation 7235 — a tariff schedule — offered as a source for peanut
butter. It named the dish, it named the United States, it corroborated honestly, and it
says nothing about how anyone makes peanut butter.

So `worthCiting()` now requires a text to describe a preparation at all, which takes
the yield to zero. `find-sources.mjs` is kept as the evidence, not as an enrichment.
Pointing different constants at these rules is cheap; believing a corpus without
measuring it is the mistake.

The route that works has no name-matching in it: a record's Wikidata item links to
articles about **the same entity** in other languages. That is
`enrich-native-language.mjs`, and it is now exhausted — of 1,800 records with no
preparation, 1,047 have no article in any language to read.

## What reading the output found instead

Every account here is lifted from a wiki, so every one passes through a stripper, and a
stripper that misses a case does not crash — it ships prose that opens
`right|thumb|300px|` and then some Hindi.

- **312 records** in the cuisine data, because `[[File:x.jpg|thumb|Caption]]` was
  rewritten by a rule meant for `[[Link|text]]`, keeping everything after the *first*
  pipe. The File namespace is `Berkas` in Indonesian and `Tập tin` in Vietnamese, so a
  list of prefixes was always going to be a list of the editions somebody thought of.
- The second fix matched `300px` and missed `300x300पिक्सेल` and `300x300پ`. Hindi and
  Persian write the pixel width in their own scripts, as they write "thumb" as
  अंगूठाकार and بندانگشتی.
- So the guard is **structural, not lexical**: running prose contains no pipe, bracket,
  brace or bare URL, in any language. A handful of genuine leads are refused. No
  account is better than an account that opens with markup.
- **435 records** carried empty brackets — "Хлеб 10 вон ()" — where a template had been
  removed from inside them.
- **1,158 recipes** in the Wikibooks cookbook had the same first-pipe bug in their
  *steps*, which is the product itself: captions glued onto the end of instructions
  ("…lumps start to form in the smooth cream.thumb|Overwhipped cream"), bare reference
  URLs, an unclosed `{{Recipe summary`.

The caption's boundary is gone from the stored text, so `repair-cookbook-markup.mjs`
re-reads those pages from source. Two traps it hit on the way, both of which had
produced confident wrong numbers first: titles from the multilingual ingest carry their
edition in front (`de:Kochbuch/…`), which en.wikibooks treats as an interwiki link and
returns empty; and those pages call their sections Zubereitung and Préparation, not
Procedure. A recipe that re-reads to an empty method is left alone and counted, never
overwritten — a repair that empties a method has deleted a recipe, not fixed one.

## The invariant that should have caught all of it

`findCatalogueViolations` now refuses reader-facing prose containing wiki markup, and
the test runs it **against the whole catalogue** rather than a two-record fixture. The
312 were invisible to 213 passing tests because every test built its own pool, and a
fixture only ever proves the fixture. It found the cookbook's 1,158 within seconds of
being pointed at real data.

Also fixed while here: `ingest-wikibooks.mjs` replaced whole records on re-run, so
re-reading a page would have deleted its country, photograph, credit and licence — six
later passes' work. It merges now. `notAFood` gained `group|holdings|industries|
enterprises`, which removes 17 beverage corporations (Wahaha, Lotte Holdings, PRAN-RFL)
and nothing else. The native-language pass now skips rows already flagged `notFood`; it
had been reading articles about restaurant chains and taxonomists in 270 cases.

### Where the markup repair finished

- **571** recipes re-read from source with the fixed parser.
- **1,950** Spanish entries fixed in place — that cookbook stores every recipe as one
  template, so `{{ing|Sal` is an ingredient with its brackets eaten, not junk. Taking
  the last pipe segment keeps the name the cook uses: `{{ing|Cebolla|Cebolleta` →
  *Cebolleta*, `फूलगोभी {{small` → *फूलगोभी*. Nothing was renamed and nothing emptied.
- **370** lines dropped as unrecoverable: an image caption glued to a step has no
  boundary left in the stored text. 160 recipes kept a usable method; **3** lost theirs
  entirely and are now name-and-place records, which the app already says plainly.
- **14** prose fields truncated at the markup boundary, backed off to the last full
  sentence; 7 kept their prose, 7 were too short to keep and were cleared.
- **61** recipes were left untouched because re-reading produced no method at all. A
  repair that empties a method has deleted a recipe, not fixed one.

**0 of 20,169 records** now carry markup in any reader-facing field, verified against
what the app actually serves rather than against what the scripts believe they wrote.

Two things stay honest about this: 113 native-language leads were *refused* by the new
structural guard, so the preparation count went down before it went up, and the Spanish
cookbook still has no section names — 102 of its recipes cannot be re-read by a section
reader at all, only cleaned in place.

# 2026-08-21 — the rows that were never marked

Looked for what was still unwalked and found something better: a bug that had been
spending requests on the same 145 rows since the day `redirects=1` was added.

## The redirect loop

`enrich-infobox.mjs` asks Wikipedia for a batch of titles with `redirects=1`, then
files each answer under the title it came back with. Ask for **Curry leaves** and the
answer is titled **Curry tree** — so the lookup missed, the row was never patched, and
because these passes mark a row as walked *only* when they patch it, it stayed pending
and was re-fetched on every run since.

It reported as "145 still pending", which reads like work remaining rather than a
fault. That is the tell worth remembering: **a count that never moves is a bug, not a
backlog.** Once unstuck those rows immediately yielded 45 preparations, 35 real places
and 60 ingredient lists that had been sitting in Wikipedia the whole time.

One layer below it, the same loop again: several records share one article. "Rau
muống", "Kangkung (vegetable)" and "Trakuon" are three records here and one page on
Wikipedia, so MediaWiki answers three questions with a single page, and filing it
against only the first left the other two pending forever. `requestedTitles` therefore
returns a *list* per answer, and the patch is computed per row — two records sharing
an article need not share a country.

Both now live in `scripts/lib/mediawiki.mjs`, because the moment a second script needed
the rule was the moment to stop copying it. `enrich-places.mjs` and
`ingest-wikibooks.mjs` had the same defect; in the ingest it was worse than a stalled
row, since a missed lookup there would have *created a second record* under the
redirect's target instead of filling in the one that exists.

`enrich-places.mjs`, once fixed, placed **384** more records below country level and
took the atlas from 1,342 to **1,504 distinct places** — the number the coverage screen
is built on, and the one that is supposed to be hard to inflate.

## And the same markup bug, a fourth time

`enrich-infobox.mjs` had its own copy of `[[Link|text]] -> text`, so
`[[File:Sheet pan.jpg|thumb|Large Sheet pan|baking…]]` opened Baklava's blurb. The
multi-row fix above then faithfully copied it to Azerbaijani pakhlava too, which is a
fair description of what that fix does: it spreads whatever the parser produces.

Fixed the same way as the others, and `preparationProse` now applies the structural
guard to its own output — no pipe, bracket, brace or bare URL — rather than trusting
the stripper above it.

Worth recording that one check here was **too** aggressive: a single `[` is legitimate
in recipe prose. "The Brown Derby Cookbook [Doubleday & Company: Garden City NY] 1949"
is a citation, and "180 ml [3/4 tasse] de crème" is a unit conversion a cook wants. The
invariant matches `[[`, not `[`, and 24 apparent problems were an artefact of a quick
check rather than anything wrong in the data.

## What is genuinely exhausted

Measured rather than assumed, and all of it free-corpus work with nothing left in it:

- **Recipe photographs.** All 3,657 photoless recipes have been walked; their pages
  carry no image. The 2,659 rows that looked unwalked already had photographs.
- **Placing recipes by country.** 2,029 cookbook recipes have no country. Their
  Wikibooks categories are technique and theme — "Easy recipes", "Broiled recipes",
  "African recipes" — not countries. A few are tantalising (`Tiv recipes` is a Nigerian
  people) but that needs an ethnicity map, and "African" is a continent, which
  `place.ts` refuses on purpose.
- **Native-language accounts.** Zero pending.
- **Infobox and risk.** Zero pending, for the first time.

The remaining gap is 883 records with no article in any language and no method
anywhere. Nothing free reaches them. That is a contributor problem now, not a scraper
problem.

## Then: reading the front page

With the scrapers exhausted, the useful thing left was to look at the app. Four faults
were visible on the first screen, and the biggest was in the position the atlas
reserves for its best evidence.

**UNESCO inscriptions were being used as dish names.** UNESCO titles the *practice*, so
the listing for ceviche is called "Practices and meanings associated with the
preparation and consumption of ceviche, an expression of Peruvian traditional cuisine".
That was the card title. `src/domain/inscription.ts` now lifts the dish out of the
inscription — the official title stays as the record's source, which is what the reader
follows to UNESCO, so nothing is lost.

**And a third of those inscriptions are not food.** Among the sixty-four: a livestock
market, a seasonal cattle drive, several saints' feasts, a fishing rite, and
"Safeguarding the carillon culture", which is church bells. Every one was showing as
Authentic — Regional at 62/100. They are refused now, with a reason each, in the shape
`isDish` and `place` already use. Twenty-seven refused, thirty-seven kept.

The rule is patterns plus an explicit refusal list rather than anything clever, because
the failure mode of guessing here is a confident wrong dish name on the most prominent
card in the app. It is deliberately *not* over-eager: "Ceremonial Keşkek tradition" is
keşkek, a wheat and meat dish, and a rule that refused it for its first word would be
the same mistake in the other direction.

**1,368 names arrived lower-case.** Wikidata labels common nouns that way — "popcorn",
"pea soup", "chimichurri" — and beside "Kozhikode Halwa" they read as a bug. Only the
first letter is touched, and only when it is a lower-case letter, so a name in a
non-Latin script is returned unchanged.

**Five regions were hedges, not places.** `place_of_origin` sometimes says "Various
claims" or "Primarily Central Europe", which the infobox reader truncated to "Primarily
Central" and printed under Kompot where a reader expects a town.

## The count that was inflated by thirty-two

Sentence-casing surfaced the real one. "falafel" became "Falafel" and met the Falafel
already in the atlas — two records, one filed under Egypt and one under "Middle Eastern
empires", which is not a country.

Seventy-nine records were filed under an origin that is not a country: Levant, Asia,
Mesoamerica, the Maghreb, the Ottoman Empire, the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, and
twenty-seven more. Each was counted as one, which is why the Elsewhere group said "32
countries" and the headline said **202**. The real number is **170**.

What was *not* done matters as much. None of those origins was narrowed to a modern
state: choosing which country the Levant is would be exactly the argument this atlas
refuses to settle for a reader. Sixty of the records are the only entry for their dish —
gefilte fish, kugel, aşure, popcorn — and a broad origin is a fact about a dish, not a
reason to delete it. They are kept, shown under "Elsewhere", and that group now says
"27 origins" instead of claiming they are countries.

Nineteen were genuine duplicates: the same dish, once under a country and once under
something vaguer. Those fold into the placed record.

The first attempt at `isCountry` tested `CONTINENTS.has(name)` and changed nothing on
screen, because `registerContinents` adds every pair the import carries — including
`["Levant", "Elsewhere"]`. A membership test said yes to precisely the values it was
meant to exclude. It tests for a real continent now.

One deliberate non-decision: 101 of 571 dish names appear in both the cuisine tree and
the Wikidata rows under **different** countries, and some of those disagreements are
real traditions rather than errors. No winner is picked. Cuisine rows simply lead the
ordering, on the grounds that they carry an article, an infobox and a preparation where
a Wikidata row carries a label — a judgement about documentation, not about origin.
Both records survive and both origin claims stay visible.
