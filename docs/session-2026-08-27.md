# 2026-08-27 — why translation was never actually happening

Ajay reported it twice, the same way both times: *"there are still translation issues in
some sections. ex. Kozhikode Halwa."* A Japanese page, an English record, no error.

Four faults in a row, each of which hid the next. Worth writing down because none of them
raised anything — the app was silent at every stage, which is why it survived a full
translation audit that only looked at output.

## 1. The endpoint was never called

`EXPO_PUBLIC_TRANSLATION_ENDPOINT` was unset, so `RemoteTranslationProvider` defaulted to
`''`, `isConfigured()` returned false, and `canTranslate()` said no. Workers AI was on, the
function answered correctly to curl, the record screen asked for a translation on mount —
and nothing was requested.

**What proved it:** the D1 `translation` table was empty and `translation_day.spent` was
sitting at exactly 5, the number of calls I had made by hand. Not "some translations
failed". *None had ever been asked for.*

Default is now the app's own `/api/translate`, the same shape `EXPO_PUBLIC_DATA_URL` uses.

## 2. The record language was hard-coded

`useTranslations` declared `language: 'en'`, entirely separate state from
`useLocale().locale`. Picking Japanese translated the chrome; the record language stayed
English. And Kozhikode Halwa *is* written in English, so:

```ts
if (dish.sourceLanguage === language) return;   // 'en' === 'en'
```

returned before any fetch. The record language now follows the app's until the reader picks
one of their own — `languageChosen`, set by `setLanguage`, keeps `LanguageBar` an explicit
override. Wired with `useLocale.subscribe` rather than into `setLocale`, so `i18n` does not
need to know a translation store exists.

## 3. The translation arrived, and was thrown away

With 1 and 2 fixed the model returned a complete, correct Japanese translation — and
`assertPreserved` rejected it.

The number check compared the whole record's digits as one bag. The method says "over 2–4
hours" in step 3; the translation also named the duration in the summary. `2,4` met
`2,2,4,4` and the reader was told the numbers had been altered. They had not.

Now per step, index-aligned. **This is stricter, not looser** — the bag could not see a
number move *between* steps, so a translation that soaked the rice for four hours and
thickened it overnight used to pass. The summary is held to its own rule: it may not lose a
number it had, and may not introduce one that is nowhere in the record.

## 4. The rejection was printed in English

`errors` held whatever the provider threw, rendered verbatim. So a reader on a Japanese page
was shown *"Translation altered the numbers in the method."* — one of the mixed-language
sections Ajay was reporting. It was one. Just not in the record.

`TranslationFailure` is now `{kind:'refused'}` or `{kind:'service', text}`. A broken
preservation rule (`PreservationError`) is a diagnostic: logged in English, shown as
`copy.translationRefused` in the reader's language. The endpoint's own refusals — a daily
limit reached — are still shown as written, because those are addressed to a reader and say
something they can act on.

## And a fifth, found while verifying

The French page read *"il est donc affiché en English"* and *"sous-titres en French"*. The
sentences were translated; the language names inside them came from the static English label
list.

`Copy` now carries `locale`, stamped by `copyFor` so no catalogue declares it, and the five
prose builders in `language.ts` and `translate.ts` call `languageNameIn(code, copy.locale)`.
Reads "en malayalam", "en français". The prompt in `translationProvider` still uses the
English name deliberately — the model reads English instructions.

## Verified on production

- `/dish/1` in Japanese: summary and steps in Japanese, no English prose, no English error.
- `/dish/1` in French, cold: the app's own `POST /api/translate` fired, a D1 row was written,
  and the day's spend went 6 → 7. This is the one that matters — it proves the app, not curl,
  drives the chain.
- Ingredients and equipment stay in Latin script. That is by construction, not a gap:
  `preservedTerms` sends them as a do-not-translate list and `assertPreserved` enforces it.

## Still open

- **Writes remain parked read-only.** Waiting on Ajay's `IDENTITY_SECRET`.
- **The 8B model leaves partial translations** — Devanagari has appeared inside French
  output. A larger model costs money, so it is Ajay's call, not mine.
- `admin.tsx`'s 32 English strings are deliberately exempt.
- Two items still unverified in a real browser: scroll reveal, loading skeleton.

# Same day, second pass — what the data was hiding

## Mobile is no longer the problem it was

The old research recorded the first photograph at **1,167px on a 375×812 phone**, 1.44
screens of chrome before anything to look at. Measured again today: **359px, 0.44
screens**, on a page 4.9 screens tall. The mobile pass landed. Motion is present too —
1,048 elements carry a transition, against the "no motion at all" the research recorded.

## Scroll reveal cannot be verified from here, and that is the honest answer

`data-reveal` reported all five shelves already `in`, which looks like the reveal firing
for everything at once. It is not. The pane's tab reports `visibilityState: 'hidden'` and
`requestAnimationFrame` never runs in it — 0 frames in 600ms — so every reading is an
artifact of a background tab rather than a measurement of the feature. Fronting the tab
does not help; the terminal is the foreground application.

Left recorded as unverified rather than claimed. `theme/reveal.ts` is built so its worst
case is a section sitting ten pixels low, which is the right thing to be relaxed about.

## A word may be in any script. It may not be in two.

Eight strings had a single letter from the wrong alphabet standing in for its lookalike.
All eight were invisible on screen and none could be searched for:

| String | The intruder |
|---|---|
| `Georgian сheese` | Cyrillic с for Latin c |
| `dolmadakiа` | Cyrillic а ending a **name** |
| `Νευροκοπίοu` | Latin u ending a Greek PGI name |
| `Weiβburgunder` | Greek beta for German eszett |
| `Jујуба` | Latin J starting a Serbian word |
| `Mυτιλήνη` | Latin M starting Mytilene |
| `Maнов мед от Странджа` | Latin Ma starting a Bulgarian name |
| `Bulgarsko kiselo mlуako` | Cyrillic у inside a transliteration |

Note the last one runs the other way — it sits in a Latin transliteration, so the
Cyrillic letter is the intruder. "Correcting" it to Cyrillic would have been the
tidy-looking wrong answer, and a rule that only knew one direction would have made it.

Three were found by hand; the other five by the invariant, including one in `gi.json`
which no test had ever read as text. **Write the check before believing the count.**

The rule is restricted to Latin against Cyrillic and Greek on purpose: those three
separate words with spaces, so one token holding two of them is a defect. CJK, kana and
Thai do not, so `Thukpa一词在藏语中` is a single token by the same measure and is correct
prose. A looser rule would have shipped a false alarm into the suite.

## A false alarm worth recording

A first scan claimed **26 records with foreign prose claiming to be English**. That was
wrong: it tested for the *presence* of a non-Latin character, and nearly all 26 were
English prose correctly citing a native term — `bap was made using gamasot (가마솥…)` —
which is what the authenticity rules ask for. Measured as a *proportion* of letters, the
real count is **zero**. The enrich script had done its job; 1,244 of 1,270 foreign-prose
records declare their language, and the rest are English.

I nearly reported 26 bugs that did not exist. Presence is not proportion.

## Partial translations are now refused

`assertTargetScript` rejects a translation containing a script that is in neither the
target language nor the source record — the "Devanagari inside French" failure. Allowed:
Latin always (every preserved term is Latin), the target's own scripts, and anything the
original already contained, so a record that quotes a Malayalam term may go on quoting
it. Under three characters it stays quiet, because one stray character is noise and
refusing a whole translation over it is worse than showing it.

Could not be reproduced on demand — both cached translations are clean (ja is Han+Kana,
fr is pure Latin) — so it is a guard against an observed weakness, not a fix for a live
repro. Said plainly rather than dressed up.

## Checked and found correct, so left alone

- **`/api/settings` returns `{}`.** The `setting` table is empty and the client falls
  back to compiled defaults. Documented behaviour, not a fault.
- **The sign-in plan is complete.** `/admin` is linked from `/support`, `SessionControl`
  is in `TopBar`, `SiteNav` and `SiteFooter`, and `/how` has its accounts section.

## The one thing still blocking Ajay's own report

`GET /api/auth/me` returns `{"available":false}` in production, so **no sign-in control
renders anywhere** — which is why he still cannot see the login. The code is right and
the omission is deliberate: a control that goes nowhere is worse than none. It needs
`GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` and `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET`, which are his to create on his own Google
account, alongside the `IDENTITY_SECRET` still outstanding.
