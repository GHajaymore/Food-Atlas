# 2026-08-27 — why translation was never actually happening

Ajay reported it twice, the same way both times: *"there are still translation issues in
some sections. ex. Kozhikode Halwa."* A Japanese page, an English record, no error.

Four faults in a row, each of which hid the next. Worth writing down because none of them
raised anything — the app was silent at every stage, which is why it survived a full
translation audit that only looked at output.

## 1. The endpoint was never called

`EXPO_PUBLIC_TRANSLATION_ENDPOINT` was unset, so `RemoteTranslationProvider` defaulted to
`''`, `isConfigured()` returned false, and `canTranslate()` said no. Workers AI was on, the
function answered correctly to curl, the record screen asked for a translation on mount —
and nothing was requested.

**What proved it:** the D1 `translation` table was empty and `translation_day.spent` was
sitting at exactly 5, the number of calls I had made by hand. Not "some translations
failed". *None had ever been asked for.*

Default is now the app's own `/api/translate`, the same shape `EXPO_PUBLIC_DATA_URL` uses.

## 2. The record language was hard-coded

`useTranslations` declared `language: 'en'`, entirely separate state from
`useLocale().locale`. Picking Japanese translated the chrome; the record language stayed
English. And Kozhikode Halwa *is* written in English, so:

```ts
if (dish.sourceLanguage === language) return;   // 'en' === 'en'
```

returned before any fetch. The record language now follows the app's until the reader picks
one of their own — `languageChosen`, set by `setLanguage`, keeps `LanguageBar` an explicit
override. Wired with `useLocale.subscribe` rather than into `setLocale`, so `i18n` does not
need to know a translation store exists.

## 3. The translation arrived, and was thrown away

With 1 and 2 fixed the model returned a complete, correct Japanese translation — and
`assertPreserved` rejected it.

The number check compared the whole record's digits as one bag. The method says "over 2–4
hours" in step 3; the translation also named the duration in the summary. `2,4` met
`2,2,4,4` and the reader was told the numbers had been altered. They had not.

Now per step, index-aligned. **This is stricter, not looser** — the bag could not see a
number move *between* steps, so a translation that soaked the rice for four hours and
thickened it overnight used to pass. The summary is held to its own rule: it may not lose a
number it had, and may not introduce one that is nowhere in the record.

## 4. The rejection was printed in English

`errors` held whatever the provider threw, rendered verbatim. So a reader on a Japanese page
was shown *"Translation altered the numbers in the method."* — one of the mixed-language
sections Ajay was reporting. It was one. Just not in the record.

`TranslationFailure` is now `{kind:'refused'}` or `{kind:'service', text}`. A broken
preservation rule (`PreservationError`) is a diagnostic: logged in English, shown as
`copy.translationRefused` in the reader's language. The endpoint's own refusals — a daily
limit reached — are still shown as written, because those are addressed to a reader and say
something they can act on.

## And a fifth, found while verifying

The French page read *"il est donc affiché en English"* and *"sous-titres en French"*. The
sentences were translated; the language names inside them came from the static English label
list.

`Copy` now carries `locale`, stamped by `copyFor` so no catalogue declares it, and the five
prose builders in `language.ts` and `translate.ts` call `languageNameIn(code, copy.locale)`.
Reads "en malayalam", "en français". The prompt in `translationProvider` still uses the
English name deliberately — the model reads English instructions.

## Verified on production

- `/dish/1` in Japanese: summary and steps in Japanese, no English prose, no English error.
- `/dish/1` in French, cold: the app's own `POST /api/translate` fired, a D1 row was written,
  and the day's spend went 6 → 7. This is the one that matters — it proves the app, not curl,
  drives the chain.
- Ingredients and equipment stay in Latin script. That is by construction, not a gap:
  `preservedTerms` sends them as a do-not-translate list and `assertPreserved` enforces it.
