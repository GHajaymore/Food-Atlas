## 4. The rest of the card, and a gate so this stops happening

Popcorn" would assert to screen-reader users precisely the thing the atlas tells everyone
else it has not established.

## 5. The atlas was selling chewing gum — `drop-non-dishes.mjs`

Found while reading bare records: **64 commercial products** filed as traditional dishes.
Cheetos, Doublemint, Juicy Fruit, Wrigley's Spearmint, Wonder Bread, Coffee-Mate, Ensure,
Soylent, Cream of Wheat, Trix, Wheaties, Dom Pérignon, Blue Nun, Cola Cao, Lao Gan Ma, the
Nescau and Toddy and Tirol lines, four Finnish cider brands. Each carried a country, a
badge and an authenticity score — so the atlas was making a claim about the traditional
preparation of a chewing gum, on a site whose one sentence about itself is *"a free atlas
of traditional dishes — where each one comes from, and who vouches for it."* A brand
vouches for itself.

All 64 were read before the rule was written. Not one was a dish, and the ones a narrower
first pattern missed were brands too — the risk here was under-matching.

The rule reads `blurb`, which on a **source** row is Wikidata's own classification of the
subject. **It is only safe there.** On a published row `blurb` can be the first 220
characters of the article's prose, and prose mentions brands in passing — *Nom yen* is a
Thai iced milk whose account names the condensed-milk brand it is usually made with. Point
`FILES` at `public/data` and this rule deletes it. That is written into the script.

A protected designation (`heritage`, `giReference`, `patRegion`) rescues a match: a PDO is
a *place's* claim on a name, which is the opposite of a company's. None of the 64 had one.

## 6. 8,883 dead ends became a link graph

Each prerendered record carried exactly one link — back into the app — so a crawler that
landed on one could read it and then had nowhere to walk. The sitemap was the only path
between any two records.

Each page now carries up to seven real links: *Also recorded in* (the atlas's own
`alsoRecordedIn` — the same dish held under another country) and *More from &lt;country&gt;*.
**52,931 internal links, none dangling, on 8,851 of 8,883 pages.** The 32 without are
legitimate: the sole indexed record filed under their country, usually a continent.

**Not linked to the place.** The obvious link is the country, but a place is browsed at
`/browse?country=Morocco`, which is client-rendered — every one of those URLs answers with
the same `index.html`. Pointing 8,883 pages at query strings serving identical HTML is the
textbook shape of a duplicate-content penalty. Links only ever go to records that were
themselves written here, so no link leads to a page with nothing on it.

`verify-prerender.mjs` now also asserts the graph: zero dangling targets, and a floor on
how many pages link anywhere. A dangling link does not 404 — the SPA catch-all answers it
with a 200 — so it is invisible from outside. Tested by pointing a link at a record that
does not exist and confirming the build fails.

## 8. Search finds plurals and alternate spellings — 2026-09-18

Measured the app's own `searchResults` over the real catalogue with queries people type.
Plurals lost most of their results, because matching was a plain substring and records
are named in the singular: "curries" 15 of 177, "samosas" 0 of 1, "empanadas" 2 of 7,
"tacos" 16 of 31. Transliterations did the same: "biriyani" 2 of 11, "kabob" 4 of 23,
"shwarma" none.

`src/domain/fold.ts` now matches every term in any of its forms:

- **`singularForms`** — "-ies"→"-y", "-es" only after s/x/z/ch/sh/o, "-s" unless "-ss",
  and no stem under four letters. Every form is the typed word or a prefix of it, so it
  can only add results. The first version stripped "-es" everywhere, turned "cookies" into
  "cooki", and matched every record mentioning cooking (39 → 106); caught by the probe.
- **`SPELLINGS`** — a short hand-written list of one dish under several spellings. Never
  related dishes, which would be the atlas deciding two traditions are the same. No
  spelling that can reach something else: not "chile" (a country), not "dal" or "ghi".

After: every singular/plural and spelling pair returns the same count. `__tests__/search-plurals.test.ts`
covers both, including the cookies/cooking and chili/Chile traps. 656 tests pass.

## 10. A correction took a week to reach anybody — 2026-09-25

Ajay, the day after 37 records stopped being labelled "Authentic": *"I saw dish's at 29 but
showing autohticated"*. The atlas was right; his browser was a week behind.

`_headers` served `/data/*` as `max-age=3600, stale-while-revalidate=604800`, with a
comment claiming an ingest "reaches everybody within the hour". Stale-while-revalidate does
not do that. It hands the **old** copy to the first visit after the hour and fetches the new
one behind it, so a correction lands one visit late — and a reader who visits once a week is
never shown anything newer than a week old.

`scripts/stamp-data-version.mjs` now hashes `public/data` (records and per-locale copy) into
`src/data/version.ts`, and every data URL carries `?v=<hash>`. Corrected data has an address
no cache has seen, so it arrives on the next visit. `/data/*` is therefore served
`immutable` for a year — correct now that the address names the contents, where before it
would have pinned readers for ever.

**It broke the preloads within minutes, exactly as that script's own comment warned.**
`inject-preload.mjs` kept writing bare `/data/x.json`, a different URL from the versioned
one the app requests, so all five sources downloaded **twice** — 2,143 KB of preload nobody
claimed. Measured on the live site, fixed, and both halves are now asserted by
`verify-prerender`:

- the data in `dist` must hash to the version in the bundle (a stale stamp would pin
  readers for a year — the very failure the old comment feared, made worse);
- every data preload must carry that version (or every file downloads twice).

Both were tested by breaking them deliberately. Live: 8 data requests, 0 unversioned, none
fetched twice.

## 12. The atlas was breaking its own rules — 2026-09-26

Started as a look at CPU cost: `buildCatalogue` is 564 ms of the 625 ms a cold load spends
on the main thread (a mid phone is roughly four times that). The profile had no dominant
hot spot, so micro-optimising correctness-critical code was the wrong trade — but it did
put `findViolations` in view, which turned out to be load-bearing rather than a dev check.

**31 shipped records violated the atlas's own invariants.** `findViolations` is applied as
a filter partway through the build, so anything that became invalid *after* that point
shipped unnoticed:

- **27 UNESCO records.** `UNESCO_BREAKDOWN`, a hand-written table, gave every inscription
  *Local source 80* and *Community validation 70* — the two dimensions `/how` tells readers
  only people can answer — for records nobody has confirmed. The same fabrication as the
  hand-typed curated scores, in a table instead of a literal. The score-withholding pass
  then stripped the total (no ingredients, no method), leaving a classified record carrying
  no number, which the same function calls a violation.
- **4 curated records** kept the "No Changes" badge after their classification dropped
  below Authentic, which that badge is reserved for.

Both now go through `assess` like everything else: inscriptions read **Unverified**, with
the human dimensions at zero and the inscription still shown and explained; the curated
badge returns if confirmations ever lift them. `UNESCO_BREAKDOWN` is deleted rather than
corrected — a second scoring table is how two answers drift apart.

Three new assertions in `plumbing.test.ts`, because the old filter only checked the middle
of the pipeline and never the finished article: no shipped record violates its invariants;
no record prints a classification without the score that justifies it; nobody gets
local-source or community points without confirmations.

660 tests. 0 violations, from 31. Verified live on /dish/500000.

## 13. Soft 404s, before inviting the crawler — 2026-10-05

Pre-launch sweep of every route a first visitor or a crawler touches. All eleven answer
200 with a description and a card image; record pages carry a static canonical and their
prerendered article; the other screens get their canonical from
`inject-canonical.mjs` at runtime, which Google executes.

One real gap: **`_redirects` answers every unmatched URL with the app shell and a 200**.
Right for a reader — a shared `/dish/4821` must reach the app, not a 404 — and wrong for a
crawler, which cannot tell "here is the page" from "here is the app, apologising". Every
typo, stale link and dead record id was an indexable page. A status code cannot fix it,
because the server does not know which paths the client router will recognise.

`src/domain/noindex.ts` marks those two screens `robots: noindex` from the screen itself,
which is where the knowledge lives, and removes the tag on unmount so a reader who lands on
a broken link and then opens a real record does not leave that record unindexable. It takes
a condition rather than being called conditionally, because the record screen decides after
its hooks have run.

**Deploy blocked:** the Cloudflare token expired mid-session (`wrangler whoami`: "auth token
has expired and could not be refreshed"). Needs `wrangler login` in an interactive terminal.
Built and committed; not live.

## 16. Saying it once — 2026-10-06

**Home, 274 → 222 words before the first dish.** `missionAskBody` was the third telling of
